# Title-level sweep for ML-based NIDS and ML security detectors: CCS 2024–25, NDSS 2024–26, EuroS&P 2024–26, RAID 2024–25, ACSAC 2024–25, AsiaCCS 2024–26

Method: I downloaded each official accepted-papers or program page, extracted every title, and keyword-filtered them with a broad regex (intrusion, anomaly, traffic, drift, shift, malware, detector, evasion, poison, backdoor, alert, explain, calibration, conformal, TLS, quantum, dataset, pitfall, provenance, DDoS, botnet, LLM, agent, robust, detect, learning and others). I then read the hits by hand. DBLP could not be used: every dblp.org, dblp.uni-trier.de and dblp.dagstuhl.de request returned an "Anubis" bot-check page or "Access Denied". Paper membership below is therefore confirmed against the official conference pages, not DBLP.

Verification legend for each summary:
- **[abs]**: summary taken from the abstract on the official paper page (NDSS, EuroS&P program) or on the open-access PDF's first page (RAID 2024).
- **[arXiv]**: summary taken from the arXiv abstract of a preprint whose title and authors match.
- **[web]**: summary taken from a secondary web source, as cited.
- **[title-only]**: the paper is confirmed on the official list but I did not retrieve its abstract. Any description is inferred from the title alone, so treat it as UNVERIFIED.

## Q1. Which accepted papers match, per venue-year? (with authors, URL, summary, tag)

### Takeaway
I found about 120 relevant papers across 14 of the 15 venue-years. AsiaCCS 2024 could not be scanned. The papers closest to a USENIX Security 2027 NIDS paper on generalization, drift and false positives are:
- KnowML (EuroS&P 2026): ML-NIDS generalization to attack variants, trained on benign traffic only, with very low FPR.
- TIPSO-GAN (NDSS 2026): zero-day evaluation with leave-one-family-out (LOFO) tests.
- PANDORA (NDSS 2026): zero-shot zero-day detection under domain shift.
- NIDP (RAID 2025): feature distribution shift in NIDS.
- Mateen (RAID 2024): benign drift in autoencoder NIDS.
- CoLD (NDSS 2026): label noise in NIDS.
- "What is the Problem Space?" (AsiaCCS 2026): realistic adversarial perturbations against ML-NIDS.
- NetMasquerade (NDSS 2026): hard-label black-box evasion of traffic detectors.
- R+R graph-NIDS re-evaluation (ACSAC 2025).
- Bad Design Smells in NIDS datasets (EuroS&P 2024).
- Rules Refine the Riddle (CCS 2024): XAI for anomaly-detection NIDS.
- tFusion (CCS 2025): label-efficient malicious traffic detection.
- Traffic models and robust encrypted-traffic training from the Tsinghua group (CCS 2025, NDSS 2024).

I found no paper at these venues on conformal or calibrated false-positive-rate guarantees under drift, and none on post-quantum TLS effects on detectors.

Tag: zero-day, drift, uncertainty (competitor, flag d). — [NDSS](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/pandora-lightweight-adversarial-defense-for-edge-iot-using-uncertainty-aware-metric-learning/)

- **NetRadar: Enabling Robust Carpet Bombing DDoS Detection** (Junchen Pan, et al., Yong Cui). [abs] Server-gateway cooperative DDoS detection, with robust training against runtime feature mismatch. - **Strategic Games and Zero Shot Attacks on Heavy-Hitter Network Flow Monitoring** (Francesco Da Dalt, Adrian Perrig). [abs] RL adversaries exceed bandwidth caps by up to 299%. Joint adversarial co-training reaches a Nash equilibrium, and zero-shot packet synthesizers succeed against 8 of 9 systems. Tag: adversarial evasion of flow monitors. — [NDSS](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/strategic-games-and-zero-shot-attacks-on-heavy-hitter-network-flow-monitoring/)
- **Indicator of Benignity: An Industry View of False Positive in Malicious Domain Detection and its Mitigation** (Daiping Liu, Danyu Sun, Zhenhua Chen, Shu Wang, Zhou Li). Tag: false-positive control (flag a-adjacent). — [NDSS](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/indicator-of-benignity-an-industry-view-of-false-positive-in-malicious-domain-detection-and-its-mitigation/)

Recovery is up to 22% shorter than with frontier LLMs. Tag: LLM automated response. — [NDSS](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/incident-response-planning-using-a-lightweight-large-language-model-with-reduced-hallucination/)

## Q2. Candidate competitors: (a) FPR guarantees or calibration under drift, (b) attacks on detector update/retraining loops, (c) post-quantum TLS effects on detectors, (d) zero-day detection with leave-attack-family-out or temporal splits

### Takeaway
At these venues there is no paper that gives formal FPR guarantees, calibration or conformal prediction under drift for NIDS. There is also no paper on post-quantum TLS effects on detectors, so those parts of a novelty claim appear clear, subject to the AsiaCCS 2024 gap. The direct competitors are on zero-day and variant generalization:
- TIPSO-GAN (NDSS 2026) uses LOFO splits.
- KnowML (EuroS&P 2026) trains on benign traffic only and reports very low FPR.
- PANDORA (NDSS 2026) reports zero-shot detection under domain shift.

The update-loop attack space is touched only indirectly:
- ADAPT (RAID 2025) mitigates self-poisoning in pseudo-labeling.
- Label-inversion drift (RAID 2025) needs its full text checked.
- Entente (NDSS 2026) evaluates FL poisoning.
- MineShark (NDSS 2025) updates itself from confirmed alarms but does not study attacks on that loop.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) FPR guarantees / calibration under drift: none found that is formal.** The nearest work:
  - KnowML reports empirical FPR ≤ 0.0137% — [arXiv 2506.19802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19802).
  - Mateen targets false alarms caused by benign drift — [RAID 2024 PDF](https://raid2024.github.io/papers/raid2024-11.pdf).
  - On Precisely Detecting Censorship Circumvention handles base-rate FPs through temporal host-level accumulation — [NDSS 2024](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/on-precisely-detecting-censorship-circumvention-in-real-world-networks/).
  - Indicator of Benignity measures and mitigates production FPs — [NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/indicator-of-benignity-an-industry-view-of-false-positive-in-malicious-domain-detection-and-its-mitigation/).
  - MineShark filters more than 99.3% of false alarms — [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/mineshark-cryptomining-traffic-detection-at-scale/).
  - Carbon Filter cuts false alerts by more than 82% — [UIUC](https://sts.cs.illinois.edu/papers/paper/CarbonFilterScalable20251019.html).
  - Ruling the Unruly addresses rule-level noise — [arXiv 2501.09808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09808).
  - Incident Response Planning proves a bound on hallucination probability, not on FPR — [NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/incident-response-planning-using-a-lightweight-large-language-model-with-reduced-hallucination/).
  - ALPHA (PAC-Bayesian active learning, AsiaCCS 2026) is title-only and UNVERIFIED as to whether it gives guarantees — [AsiaCCS 2026 cycle 1](https://asiaccs2026.cse.iitkgp.ac.in/cycle-1-papers/).
- **(b) Attacks on detector update/retraining loops: no direct NIDS paper found.** The nearest work:
  - ADAPT explicitly addresses "catastrophic self-poisoning" in drift-time pseudo-labeling — [arXiv 2507.08597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08597).
  - "Detecting and Adapting to Stealthy Label-Inversion Drifts" is title-only; the word "stealthy" suggests an adversarial angle — [RAID 2025 list](https://raid2025.github.io/accepted_open.html).
  - Entente evaluates FL poisoning tailored to graph NIDS — [NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/entente-cross-silo-intrusion-detection-on-network-log-graphs-with-federated-learning/).
  - PBP purifies backdoored malware classifiers — [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/pbp-post-training-backdoor-purification-for-malware-classifiers/).
  - Density Boosts Everything handles poisoning and drift jointly — [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/density-boosts-everything-a-one-stop-strategy-for-improving-performance-robustness-and-sustainability-of-malware-detectors/).
  - MineShark's self-improving updates driven by confirmed alarms are a loop an attacker could target, but the paper does not study such attacks — [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/mineshark-cryptomining-traffic-detection-at-scale/).
- **(c) Post-quantum TLS effects on detectors: none found.** The closest analogue is the effect of protocol evolution, TLS 1.2 to TLS 1.3, on C2 detectors. That paper recovers more than 93% detection on TLS 1.3 while training only on TLS 1.2 — [RAID 2024 PDF](https://raid2024.github.io/papers/raid2024-31.pdf). The post-quantum papers found are protocol designs, not detector or measurement studies: PQConnect at [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/pqconnect-automated-post-quantum-end-to-end-tunnels/), and PQ DNSSEC and quantum-safe DNSSEC at AsiaCCS 2025/2026.
- **(d) Zero-day detection with leave-family-out or temporal splits:**
  - TIPSO-GAN reports 92.3 F1 in LOFO tests and 79–83 F1 cross-dataset on CIC-IDS2018, CICAPT-IIoT2024 and CIC-DDoS2019 — [NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/tipso-gan-malicious-network-traffic-detection-using-a-novel-optimized-generative-adversarial-network/).
  - KnowML generalizes to unseen attack variants with a benign-only training set — [arXiv 2506.19802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19802).
  - PANDORA reports zero-shot accuracy up to 99% under domain shift and 10-shot F1 0.971 on CICIDS2017 — [NDSS 2026](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/pandora-lightweight-adversarial-defense-for-edge-iot-using-uncertainty-aware-metric-learning/).
  - 5G-Spector scales to 11 unknown attacks — [NDSS 2024](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/5g-spector-an-o-ran-compliant-layer-3-cellular-attack-detection-service/).
  - Cross-Regional Malware Detection uses temporal ordering — [RAID 2024 PDF](https://raid2024.github.io/papers/raid2024-3.pdf).
  - Revisiting Concept Drift uses real 2024 drifted malware — [NDSS 2025](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/revisiting-concept-drift-in-windows-malware-detection-adaptation-to-real-drifted-malware-with-minimal-samples/).
  - No Need for Details trains on normal traffic only and tests transfer — [RAID 2024 PDF](https://raid2024.github.io/papers/raid2024-41.pdf).
  - NIDP addresses feature distribution shift in NIDS; its split protocol is unknown (title-only) — [RAID 2025 list](https://raid2025.github.io/accepted_open.html).

### Inferences
- The strongest overlap risks for a USENIX 2027 NIDS novelty claim are:
  - KnowML: same problem framing (anomaly NIDS generalizing to variants at low FPR); it shows incremental and contrastive learning frameworks fail.
  - TIPSO-GAN: LOFO zero-day protocol on CIC datasets.
  - PANDORA: zero-shot detection under domain shift with uncertainty-aware embeddings.
  - Mateen: online adaptation to benign drift with 1% labeling.
- The paper should state explicitly how its claimed contributions differ from these four.
- A claim of a "first FPR guarantee under drift" for NIDS appears defensible against these venues, but ALPHA (PAC-Bayesian) and label-inversion drift (RAID 2025) need full-text checks first.

### Gaps
- I could not read full papers to confirm their exact split protocols, for example whether KnowML or PANDORA use leave-family-out splits. Only abstracts were read.
