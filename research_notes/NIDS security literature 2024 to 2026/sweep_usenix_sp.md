# Title-level sweep of USENIX Security 2024/2025/2026 and IEEE S&P 2024/2025/2026 for ML-based NIDS and ML security-detector papers

Method and coverage (applies to every section below):
- I scanned all six official lists in full. USENIX: technical-sessions pages for [USENIX Sec '24](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/technical-sessions) (417 unique paper/talk pages), [USENIX Sec '25](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/technical-sessions) (455 entries, keynotes included), [USENIX Sec '26](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/technical-sessions) (378 unique pages; the [cycle-1 accepted list](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/cycle1-accepted-papers) is a subset; the cycle-2 accepted-papers URL returned an empty shell). S&P: [sp2024 accepted papers](https://sp2024.ieee-security.org/accepted-papers.html) (261 titles), [sp2025](https://sp2025.ieee-security.org/accepted-papers.html) (255), [sp2026](https://sp2026.ieee-security.org/accepted-papers.html) (254).
- First pass: keyword match on all ~1,550 titles (intrusion, anomaly, traffic, drift, continual, malware, detector, alert, SOC, provenance, TLS, post-quantum, conformal, calibration, explainab*, dataset, evasion, poison, backdoor, certified, pre-train, DDoS, DNS, and others). Second pass: a broader title filter (detect, network, threat, learning, graph, agent, audit, adversarial, SoK, LLM, and others). Third pass: abstract-text match on USENIX abstracts for intrusion detection, anomaly detection, concept drift, malicious/encrypted traffic, zero-day, conformal, false positive rate and similar terms. I read every flagged title by hand.
- Verification: USENIX summaries come from the official abstracts on each presentation page. **The S&P accepted-paper pages list titles and authors only, with no abstracts.** For S&P, I took abstracts from arXiv where a matching preprint existed. Where none was found, the paper is confirmed on the official list but its summary is marked **UNVERIFIED (title-level only)**.
- DBLP (dblp.org/db/conf/uss, /sp) was blocked by a bot-challenge page (HTTP 202 / "Making sure you're not a bot"). OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar APIs were rate-limited, and a web-search quota ran out partway through. **No venue-year was left unscanned**: all six primary lists loaded. The gaps are S&P abstracts only.

## Q1/Q2. Relevant accepted papers per venue-year, with verified extraction

### Takeaway
Every venue-year has relevant work. The densest clusters are: (i) provenance-graph host IDS (about 12 papers); (ii) adversarial robustness and certification of traffic and network classifiers (PANTS, CertTA, plus the S&P 2026 IDS-evasion paper); (iii) SOC alerts, LLM triage and automated response (USENIX 2026 especially); and (iv) evaluation pitfalls and dataset critiques (provenance IDS, encrypted traffic classifiers, endpoint logs, sampling bias, DRL4Sec). I found no paper at these six venue-years that measures post-quantum TLS effects on network detectors. Only one paper offers conformal-prediction guarantees under poisoning (EnCP, S&P 2026), and it is not NIDS-specific.

### Cited Findings

#### USENIX Security 2024
- **True Attacks, Attack Attempts, or Benign Triggers? An Empirical Measurement of Network Alerts in a Security Operations Center.** Limin Yang, Zhi Chen, Chenkai Wang, Zhenning Zhang, Sushruth Booma, Phuong Cao, Constantin Adam, Alexander Withers, Zbigniew Kalbarczyk, Ravishankar K. Iyer, Gang Wang (UIUC/NCSA/IBM). The paper analyses 4 years of real SOC network alerts (115M alerts, 2018–2022) against ground truth for 227 successful attacks. It finds 24K–134K alerts/day, of which only 0.01% are tied to true attacks; 27% are "attack attempts" and 49% are "benign triggers". Rare or abnormal alert patterns could help isolate true attacks. Tag: false-positive control / alert triage / evaluation. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/yang-limin)
- **Detecting and Mitigating Sampling Bias in Cybersecurity with Unlabeled Data.** Saravanan Thirumuruganathan, Fatih Deniz, Issa Khalil, Ting Yu, Mohamed Nabeel, Mourad Ouzzani. The paper attributes the gap between research and deployment performance to sampling bias (train/production distribution shift). It proposes domain-discrimination-based detection and domain-adaptation-inspired mitigation that use unlabeled production data without modifying the classifier. Evaluated on malware, web-domain and **intrusion detection** datasets, it improves F-score by 10–16 points (AndroZoo: 0.83→0.937). Tag: distribution shift / dataset pitfalls. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/thirumuruganathan)
- **Learning with Semantics: Towards a Semantics-Aware Routing Anomaly Detection System.** Yihao Chen, Qilei Yin, Qi Li, Zhuotao Liu, Ke Xu, Yi Xu, Mingwei Xu et al. (Tsinghua). The system uses BEAM, a network-representation-learning model of AS routing roles, for interpretable BGP anomaly detection. On 18 RouteViews datasets (11B announcements) it detects all confirmed anomalies with at most 5 false alarms per 180M announcements. A one-month ISP deployment found 497 true anomalies at 1.65 false alarms/day. Tag: network anomaly detection / FP control / explainability. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/chen-yihao)
- **You Cannot Escape Me: Detecting Evasions of SIEM Rules in Enterprise Networks.** Rafael Uetz, Marco Herzog, Louis Hackländer, Simon Schwarz, Martin Henze. The authors show that adversaries can easily evade almost half of widespread SIEM rules. AMIDES ("adaptive misuse detection") uses ML to compare events to rules and to known-benign events. On 4 weeks of enterprise SIEM events and more than 500 hand-crafted evasions, it detects most evasions "without any false alerts". Tag: evasion of detectors / FP control. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/uetz)
- **Enhancing Network Attack Detection with Distributed and In-Network Data Collection System (ISDC).** S. M. M. Mirnajafizadeh, A. R. Sethuram, David Mohaisen, DaeHun Nyang, Rhongho Jang. ISDC is in-network serverless flow-data collection for ML/DL network defense, with sublinear flow prioritisation and task migration. In covert-channel and DoS/DDoS use cases it achieves higher flow coverage and feature accuracy, and it fixes a "model self-poisoning" issue caused by duplicated or fragmented flow measurements. Tag: NIDS data pipeline / poisoning (measurement-induced). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/mirnajafizadeh)
- **MAGIC: Detecting Advanced Persistent Threats via Masked Graph Representation Learning.** Zian Jia, Yun Xiong, Yuhong Nan, Yao Zhang, Jinjing Zhao et al. MAGIC is self-supervised APT detection that learns benign provenance behaviour through masked graph representation learning and flags outliers. It is "specially designed to handle concept drift with a model adaption mechanism" and was evaluated on three datasets. Tag: provenance IDS / unseen-attack detection / concept drift. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/jia-zian)
- **Fingerprinting Obfuscated Proxy Traffic with Encapsulated TLS Handshakes.** Diwen Xue, Michalis Kallitsis, Amir Houmansadr, Roya Ensafi. This is protocol-agnostic proxy detection: similarity-based classifiers identify encapsulated TLS handshakes inside encrypted streams. Deployed at an ISP, it reliably detects proxies (even with padding or multiple encapsulation layers) with minimal collateral damage. Tag: encrypted traffic classification. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/xue-fingerprinting)
- **How does Endpoint Detection use the MITRE ATT&CK Framework?** Apurva Virkud, Muhammad Adil Inam, Andy Riddle, Jason Liu, Gang Wang, Adam Bates. An analysis of EDR/SIEM detection rules' ATT&CK coverage. Many techniques are unrealizable as rules, and covering a technique does not consistently mean covering the same real threats. Tag: detector evaluation pitfalls. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/virkud)
- **A Wolf in Sheep's Clothing: Practical Black-box Adversarial Attacks for Evading Learning-based Windows Malware Detection in the Wild (MalGuise).** Xiang Ling, Zhiyu Wu, Bin Wang et al. MalGuise applies a semantics-preserving call-based redividing transformation to control-flow graphs, searched with MCTS. It achieves more than 95% attack success against three learning-based detectors and up to 74.97% against five AV products. Tag: adversarial evasion of security detectors. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/ling)
- **SAIN: Improving ICS Attack Detection Sensitivity via State-Aware Invariants.** Syed Ghazanfar Abbas, Muslum Ozgur Ozmen, Abdulellah Alsaheel, Arslan Khan, Z. Berkay Celik, Dongyan Xu. SAIN derives state-specific invariants from PLC program analysis so that stealthy attacks cannot hide inside loose bounds. Against 17 attacks it reaches 2% FPR with 3% overhead. Tag: ICS anomaly/intrusion detection (non-ML invariants). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/abbas)
- **ChainPatrol: Balancing Attack Detection and Classification with Performance Overhead for Service Function Chains Using Virtual Trailers.** Momen Oqaily et al. (Concordia/Ericsson). Tenant-side detection and classification of SFC forwarding attacks, using side-channel watermark "virtual trailers": 100% detection and 70% classification accuracy with near-zero traffic overhead. Tag: network attack detection (peripheral, not ML-centric). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/oqaily)
- **Uncovering the Limits of Machine Learning for Automatic Vulnerability Detection.** Niklas Risse, Marcel Böhme. ML4VD models overfit to unrelated features, generalize poorly out of distribution, and cannot distinguish vulnerable functions from their patched versions. The paper proposes a new benchmarking methodology. Tag: evaluation pitfalls (methodology transferable; not NIDS). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/risse)
- Peripheral/contextual USENIX '24 (confirmed on list; relevance weak):
  - **Unveiling the Hunter-Gatherers: Exploring Threat Hunting Practices and Challenges in Cyber Defense** (Badva, Ramokapane, Pantano, Rashid): interviews with 22 threat hunters. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/badva)
  - **An Interview Study on Third-Party Cyber Threat Hunting Processes in the U.S. DHS** (Maxam, Davis). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/maxam)
  - **Stop, Don't Click Here Anymore: Boosting Website Fingerprinting By Considering Sets of Subpages** (Mitseva, Panchenko): encrypted-traffic classification of Tor traffic. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/mitseva)
  - **Verify your Labels! Trustworthy Predictions and Datasets via Confidence Scores** (Krauß, Stang, Dmitrienko): LabelTrust confidence scores for detecting poisoned samples and predictions. Generic ML; possibly useful for label-noise and poisoning defenses. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/krauss-verify)
  - **CalcuLatency: Leveraging Cross-Layer Network Latency Measurements to Detect Proxy-Enabled Abuse** (Ramesh, Winter, Korman, Ensafi). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/ramesh)
  - The phishing-detector papers (PhishDecloaker; "Less Defined Knowledge and More True Alarms"; KnowPhish; "It Doesn't Look Like Anything to Me" diffusion evasion of visual phishing detectors) are security detectors but not network/NIDS. They are listed on the [USENIX '24 program](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/technical-sessions).

#### USENIX Security 2025
- **Robustifying ML-powered Network Classifiers with PANTS.** Minhao Jin, Maria Apostolaki (Princeton). PANTS is a white-box framework that combines adversarial ML with SMT solvers to generate *realizable*, semantics-preserving adversarial inputs for ML network classifiers, **including intrusion detection**. Built into iterative adversarial training, it finds adversarial inputs 70% / 2x more often (median) than Amoeba and BAP, and improves robustness by 52.7%, even against out-of-scope attackers, without losing accuracy. Tag: adversarial evasion of NIDS / traffic models. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/jin-minhao)
- **CertTA: Certified Robustness Made Practical for Learning-Based Traffic Analysis.** Jinzhu Yan, Zhuotao Liu, Yuyang Xie, Shiyu Liang, Lin Liu, Ke Xu. The first certified robustness against *multi-modal* traffic perturbations (packet-length padding, timing delays, dummy packet insertion). It uses attack-aware randomized smoothing and gives stronger guarantees than the state of the art across 6 traffic-analysis models and 2 datasets. Tag: certified robustness for network models. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/yan-jinzhu)
- **Sometimes Simpler is Better: A Comprehensive Analysis of State-of-the-Art Provenance-Based Intrusion Detection Systems.** Tristan Bilot, Baoxiang Jiang, Zefeng Li, Nour El Madhoun, Khaldoun Al Agha, Anis Zouaoui, Thomas Pasquier. The authors reimplemented 8 PIDSs in a unified framework and found 9 shortcomings that block deployment. A simple neural network matches the state of the art on 5 of 7 DARPA datasets. They release the framework and ground-truth datasets. Tag: evaluation pitfalls / dataset critique (host IDS). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/bilot)
- **ORTHRUS: Achieving High Quality of Attribution in Provenance-based Intrusion Detection Systems.** Baoxiang Jiang, Tristan Bilot, et al., Xueyuan Han, Thomas Pasquier. The paper names "Quality of Attribution" (analyst effort, including dismissing false alarms) as the adoption barrier. A GNN encoder with dependency analysis reduces nodes needing inspection by orders of magnitude versus 5 IDSs. Tag: provenance IDS / alert triage / FP burden. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/jiang-baoxiang)
- **TAPAS: An Efficient Online APT Detection with Task-guided Process Provenance Graph Segmentation and Analysis.** Bo Zhang, Yansong Gao, Changlong Yu, Boyu Kuang, Zhi Zhang, Hyoungshick Kim, Anmin Fu. Spatial and temporal graph-dimensionality reduction for online APT detection on DARPA TC and OpTC: up to 1806x less storage, 99.99% accuracy, 12.78 s/GB. Tag: provenance IDS. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-bo-tapas)
- **GeCos Replacing Experts: Generalizable and Comprehensible Industrial Intrusion Detection.** Konrad Wolsing, Eric Wagner, Luisa Lux, Klaus Wehrle, Martin Henze. GeCo builds an industrial IDS from automatically mined, comprehensible state-space models of benign behaviour. It generalizes across industrial domains and performs on par with expert rules. Tag: anomaly IDS / explainability. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wolsing)
- **AutoLabel: Automated Fine-Grained Log Labeling for Cyber Attack Dataset Generation.** Yihao Peng, Tongxin Zhang, Jieshao Lai, Yuxuan Zhang, Yiming Wu, Hai Wan, Xibin Zhao. Automated attack-subgraph labeling that correlates application and **traffic logs** with audit logs. It reaches 100% labeling accuracy in 29 scenarios (25 CVEs plus MITRE Sandworm) and has produced more than 580 benchmark datasets. Tag: dataset generation / labeling quality. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/peng-yihao)
- **Persistent Backdoor Attacks in Continual Learning.** Zhen Guo, Abhinav Kumar, Reza Tourani. Two backdoors (Blind Task, Latent Task) persist through continual-learning updates with minimal adversarial influence and evade SentiNet and I-BAU. Evaluated on vision-style triggers, not security data. Tag: poisoning of update/retraining loops. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/guo-zhen)
- **Fighting Fire with Fire: Continuous Attack for Adversarial Android Malware Detection (HagDe).** Yinyuan Zhang et al. Detects adversarial examples against Android malware detectors from the disproportionate loss increase under small gradient-ascent perturbations. Defense effectiveness is 88.5% on AdvDroidZero and 90.7% on BagAmmo. Tag: adversarial robustness of malware detectors. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zhang-yinyuan)
- **VAPD: An Anomaly Detection Model for PDF Malware Forensics with Adversarial Robustness.** Side Liu, Jiang Ming, Yilin Zhou, Jianming Fu, Guojun Peng. A reconstruction-based anomaly detector trained on benign PDFs only: 99.54% accuracy, robustness under 4 feature- and problem-space attack frameworks, at 4.8% of the state of the art's training cost. Tag: anomaly detection / adversarial robustness. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/liu-side)
- **Gotta Detect 'Em All: Fake Base Station and Multi-Step Attack Detection in Cellular Networks (FBSDetector).** Kazi Samin Mubasshir, Imtiaz Karim, Elisa Bertino. ML on layer-3 traces (stateful LSTM with attention plus graph learning) with new FBSAD/MSAD datasets. It detects fake base stations at 96% accuracy and 2.96% FPR, and multi-step attacks at 86% accuracy and 3.28% FPR. Deployed as a mobile app. Tag: ML network intrusion detection (cellular). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/mubasshir)
- **Lemon: Network-Wide DDoS Detection with Routing-Oblivious Per-Flow Measurement.** Wenhao Wu, Zhenyu Li, et al. Routing-oblivious sketches in the data plane (Bmv2/Tofino) for network-wide DDoS detection. Tag: network attack detection (sketch-based, not ML). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wu-wenhao)
- **Preventing Artificially Inflated SMS Attacks through Large-Scale Traffic Inspection.** Jun Ho Huh et al. (Samsung). Feature engineering on 9.4M SMS request logs: 89.6% / 91.1% recall at 0.2% / 0.1% FPR. Tag: traffic-based abuse detection / FP control (peripheral). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/huh)
- **Ares: Comprehensive Path Hijacking Detection via Routing Tree.** Yinxiang Tao et al. BGP path-hijack detection: 97.2–99.3% detection, 1.06% FPR, 2.31 alerts/hour Internet-wide. Tag: routing anomaly detection. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/tao)
- **Censorship Evasion with Unidentified Protocol Generation (UPGen).** Ryan Wails, Rob Jansen, Aaron Johnson, Micah Sherr. Generated encrypted protocols that ML traffic classifiers cannot block without collateral damage. Tag: evasion of traffic classifiers. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wails)
- **Predictive Response Optimization: Using Reinforcement Learning to Fight Online Social Network Abuse.** Garrett Wilson et al. (Meta). RL picks response actions (ban, block, CAPTCHA, collect evidence) instead of binary classification. Deployed on Instagram and Facebook, it reduced abuse volume by 59% and 4.5% respectively. Tag: automated response (non-network). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wilson)
- Peripheral USENIX '25:
  - **Expert Insights into Advanced Persistent Threats: Analysis, Attribution, and Challenges** (Saha et al.) — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/saha)
  - "SoK: Automated TTP Extraction from CTI Reports – Are We There Yet?", "The Ransomware Decade", and "High Stakes, Low Certainty: … Indicators of Compromise in Ransomware Attribution" are confirmed on the [USENIX '25 program](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/technical-sessions).

#### USENIX Security 2026
- **Retrofit: Continual Learning with Controlled Forgetting for Binary Security Detection and Analysis.** Yiling He, Junchi Lei, Hongyu She, Shuo Shao, Xinran Zheng, Yiping Liu, Zhan Qin, Lorenzo Cavallaro. Continual learning with two-teacher knowledge aggregation to control forgetting. **For malware detection under temporal drift** it raises the retention score from 20.2% to 38.6% over CL baselines and exceeds the oracle on new data. Tag: concept drift / continual learning for security detectors (transferable to NIDS). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/he-yiling)
- **Tracegram: Framing Trace-Level Traffic Analysis with Temporally-Aware Multiple Instance Learning.** Jian Qu, Yuchen Zhang, Jialong Zhang, Jianfeng Li, Xiaobo Ma (XJTU). Trace-level (multi-flow) traffic analysis as multiple-instance learning, combining per-flow encoders with temporally-aware aggregation. Better or comparable to the state of the art on 4 public datasets; key-flow attribution on DAPT APT traces aligns with attack phases. Tag: traffic classification / NIDS / explainability. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/qu)
- **Context Contamination in LLM Analysis of Network Security Logs: Poison with Passive Prompt Injection and Mitigation Evaluation (LogInject).** Rabimba Karanjai, Yang Lu, Hemanth Hegadehalli Madhavarao, Lei Xu, Weidong Shi. Prompt-injection payloads planted in log fields hijack SOC LLMs used for summarization and **alert triage**. The benchmark has 12,847 entries (2,569 adversarial); fragmented payloads succeed 76.4% of the time. Layered defenses cut attacks by 90.4%, leaving 8.4% residual. Tag: LLM alert triage / attacks on detection pipeline. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/karanjai)
- **When AIOps Become "AI Oops": Subverting LLM-driven IT Operations via Telemetry Manipulation (AIOpsDoom / AIOpsShield).** Dario Pasquini, Evgenios M. Kornaropoulos, Giuseppe Ateniese, Omer Akgul, Athanasios Theocharis, Petros Efstathopoulos. Adversaries inject telemetry through error-inducing requests to mislead autonomous LLM agents that perform anomaly detection, diagnosis and remediation. AIOpsShield sanitizes telemetry and blocks the attacks. Tag: agentic automated response / attacks on detector inputs. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/pasquini)
- **Cutting the Fuse: Actionable APT Attack Blocking in Provenance-based IDS (ProvX).** Weiheng Wu, Wei Qiao, Teng Li, Yebo Feng, Zhuo Ma, Jianfeng Ma et al. Counterfactual-explanation logic turns PIDS alert floods into a minimal "core edge list" for blocking. Evaluated on DARPA; it includes a preliminary alert-feedback loop to guide model optimization in adversarial scenarios. Tag: explainability / automated response / alert reduction. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/wu-weiheng)
- **DUPIN: Attack Learning Is Still Needed! Demonstrating Few-Shot after Unsupervised Pretraining Is A Nimble Forensics Learner.** Chanwoo Bae, Hailun Ding, Shiqing Ma, Xiangyu Zhang. Argues that benign-only self-supervised outlier detection yields non-trivial FPs. Pretrains on 7.3TB of provenance audit logs (38–52 days), then few-shot fine-tunes on labeled attacks; evaluated on 25 APT campaigns from 4 data sources. Tag: pre-trained/foundation model for security logs / FP. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/bae)
- **Facade: High-Precision Insider Threat Detection Using Deep Contextual Anomaly Detection.** Alex Kantchelian, Casper Neo, Ryan Stevens, et al., Elie Bursztein, Massimiliano Poletto (Google). A self-supervised, contrastive, benign-only contextual anomaly detector deployed at Google since 2018 over document, SQL and HTTP/RPC logs. FPR is below 0.01% (0.0003% for single rogue events). Tag: anomaly detection / extreme low-FPR operation. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/kantchelian)
- **SoK: The Pitfalls of Deep Reinforcement Learning for Cybersecurity.** Shae McFadden, Myles Foley, Elizabeth Bates, Ilias Tsingenopoulos, Sanyam Vyas, Vasilios Mavroudis, Chris Hicks, Fabio Pierazzi et al. Defines 11 methodological pitfalls; 66 DRL4Sec papers average more than 5 pitfalls each. Controlled experiments cover autonomous cyber defense and adversarial malware. Tag: evaluation pitfalls / automated response. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/mcfadden)
- **You Know Why, but Still Rely: The Impact of Explainable AI on Trust, Task Load, and Performance in Cybersecurity Decision-Making.** Neele Roch, Hannah Sievers, Noé Zufferey, Verena Zimmermann (ETH). A between-subjects study (N=139) on malicious-domain blocking. XAI did not raise trust (domain experts trusted less after it), did not raise task load, and did not improve performance. Tag: explainability of security detectors. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/roch)
- **Credible Threat Detection? Measuring Contribution Dynamics and Quality Control in a Crowdsourced Threat Detection Ecosystem.** Apurva Virkud, Gang Wang, Adam Bates. A study of the Sigma rule repository: continual false-positive tuning from user feedback, cross-vendor divergence, and evasion risk introduced by contributions. Tag: detection-rule FP tuning / rule update risk. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/virkud)
- **From Texts to Rules: Generating Sigma Rules with Large Language Models from Cyber Threat Reports (SIGMERGE).** Yongxin Cai, Jing Qiu, Qingming Li, Du Cheng, Lei Chen. LLM-generated Sigma rules from CTI shell commands, evaluated against 16 baselines and 13 LLMs; 4 rules were accepted upstream. Tag: LLM for detection engineering. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/cai)
- **Towards Practical Few-shot Multi-tab Website Fingerprinting (MMF).** Lin Liu, Ziling Wei, Zhuotao Liu, Xinhao Deng, Zixuan Dong, Shuhui Chen. Few-shot presence detection over interleaved encrypted Tor traffic; detects *previously unseen* websites from limited traces, with up to 300% Novel Precision@k gain at 5-shot. Tag: encrypted traffic classification / open-set. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/liu-lin)
- **Estimating the Amount of Script-generated Traffic in a Mixture.** Cormac Herley (MSR). Upper-bounds the bot fraction α in clean/bot mixtures through rank-one matrix estimation, with confidence intervals; validated on Twitter. Tag: traffic/bot estimation with statistical guarantees (peripheral). — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/herley)

## Q3. Candidate competitors: (a) FPR guarantees/calibration under drift, (b) attacks on detector update/retraining loops, (c) post-quantum TLS effects on detectors, (d) zero-day detection with leave-family-out or temporal splits

### Takeaway
I found **no paper at these six venue-years that combines FPR guarantees with drift for NIDS, and none on post-quantum TLS effects on detectors**. The closest are partial: EnCP (conformal plus poisoning, generic) for (a); Test-Time Poisoning against TTA, Persistent Backdoors in Continual Learning, and Retrofit's drift setting for (b) and temporal drift; and KAIROS, MAGIC, Jbeil, MANTIS and MMF for (d). Whether any of them use explicit leave-attack-family-out splits is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- (a) **EnCP** (S&P 2026): a conformal predictor with robustness guarantees against data poisoning. It is generic ML; no drift or NIDS evidence was found. — [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/YangLFSW26/authors)

### Inferences
- A USENIX 2027 claim of "first FPR-guaranteed (conformal/calibrated) NIDS under drift" appears not to be pre-empted at these six venue-years. EnCP must be cited and distinguished: it is poisoning-robust conformal prediction, while the claim concerns drift-time FPR control. This judgment rests on titles plus the available abstracts. EnCP's full text should be checked.
- A claim about attacks on NIDS retraining/update loops would overlap conceptually with Test-Time Poisoning (S&P '24) and Persistent Backdoors in CL (USENIX '25). It should be differentiated through the network-traffic setting and the threat model (e.g., label or feedback poisoning during periodic retraining).
- A post-quantum TLS × detector-impact angle looks open at these venues.
