## Q3. Page limits, template, anonymization, double-blind

### Takeaway
- Main body: 13 pages at submission. References and appendices do not count and have no length limit, but reviewers need not read optional appendices.
- Camera-ready: 20 pages total, including all appendices and references.
- Format: the mandatory USENIX 2027 LaTeX template.
- Review is strictly double-blind.

### Cited Findings
- "The main body of the submission must not exceed 13 pages excluding References and any appendices." — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- "At submission time, there is no limit on the length of the bibliography and optional appendices." Reviewers "are not required to read any optional appendices." — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- "Once accepted, papers must be reformatted to fit in 20 pages, including any 'Ethical Considerations' or 'Open Science' appendices," as well as the bibliography and other appendices. Page limits must be met "through the modification of content alone." — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- Template: https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/usenixsecurity2027_latex_templates.zip — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
  - "U.S. letter-sized pages in two-column format in 10-point Times Roman type on 12-point leading," with a 7" x 9" text block. Submissions must be PDF.
  - Space-saving tricks are forbidden ("negative vspaces, savetrees, titlesec, removing author blocks"), as is changing defaults such as fonts. These "may result in rejection."
  - Papers must be intelligible in grayscale.
- Anonymity — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
  - "Papers must be submitted for anonymous review: no author names or affiliations may appear on the title page."
  - Refer to your own prior work in the third person. Blind the citation only if that is infeasible.
  - Artifact links must not reveal identities or track reviewers.
  - Papers not properly anonymized "may be rejected without review."
- Preprints, talks, and vulnerability disclosures are allowed during review. Timing should not "appear to intentionally aim to inform reviewers of their identity." — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- Other submission rules — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
  - "Simultaneous submission of the same work to multiple venues with archived proceedings" is prohibited.
  - Overlapping work under submission elsewhere must be disclosed and cited anonymously, with non-anonymous versions sent to the chairs. Failure to explain overlap "will be grounds for rejection."
  - SoK titles must be prefixed "SoK:".
  - Conflicts must be declared in HotCRP.
- "Each author can submit at most seven papers per cycle." If an author exceeds this, the chairs keep the seven with the lowest submission numbers. — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)

### Inferences
- At submission, the 13-page body is the binding constraint. For camera-ready, plan the whole paper (body, references, Open Science appendix, any ethics appendix, and other appendices) to fit in 20 pages.
- Anything important must be in the 13-page body, because reviewers may skip appendices.

### Gaps
- The 2027 CFP does not say where the appendices must sit relative to the references. (The 2026 CFP required the Ethics appendix after the main body and before the references.) NOT FOUND for 2027.

## Q4. Open Science policy, Open Science Appendix, artifacts

### Takeaway
- Every submission must include an Open Science Appendix. It either lists the artifacts with anonymous, non-tracking links, or explicitly justifies why they cannot be provided.
- Artifacts are due 3 days after the paper and are then frozen.
- For accepted papers, an availability check on a permanent archive is mandatory. Functional and Reproduced badges are optional.

### Cited Findings
- "We encourage all authors to release all artifacts (source code, datasets, models, benchmarks, scripts, etc.) needed to evaluate the paper's core contributions, to the extent possible while maintaining data confidentiality and industry protections." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- "Therefore, all USENIX Security '27 submissions must include an Open Science Appendix describing the artifacts and how to access them or an explicit description of why artifacts cannot be provided." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Anonymous links — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
  - "Artifacts must be shared through anonymous links with no tracking mechanisms."
  - Authors must ensure no identifying information is exposed ("usernames, organization names, commit history").
  - Recommended host: https://anonymous.4open.science with conference ID "SEC27". "Files must remain accessible for the entire evaluation period, until the shepherd approval deadline."
- "Anonymous URLs should be included in the paper's Open Science Appendix. After the submission deadline, URLs are final, but a 3-day grace period is provided to anonymize and upload artifacts. During this period, the paper itself cannot be modified. Once the grace period ends, artifacts are considered frozen and must not be updated." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- If artifacts cannot be shared — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
  - "We expect all submitted artifacts to be available publicly on paper acceptance," but some "cannot be shared publicly e.g., due to licensing restrictions, adversarial risk, or harm to study subjects."
  - "If at all possible, submit the artifacts and note in the Open Science Appendix those which will not be made public."
  - "If submission of artifacts is not possible (e.g., due to NDA or legal constraints), the Open Science Appendix must explain such omissions explicitly."
- Acceptance "is conditional on their public availability" for artifacts declared public. "Camera-ready paper versions will include an Open Science Appendix that includes a non-anonymous, stable artifact link." Artifacts are treated with the same confidentiality as the manuscript. — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- Artifact Evaluation — [Call for Artifacts](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-artifacts)
  - Availability verification is mandatory for conditionally accepted and shepherded papers. Artifacts must be on "a platform that supports permanent access" (Zenodo, FigShare, Dryad, Software Heritage, or an institutional repository). Personal websites and GitHub are not accepted.
  - The Open Science discussion must "clearly enumerate all artifacts that are made available" and refer to results in the main body.
  - Functional and Reproduced badges are optional.
- Withheld artifacts — [Call for Artifacts](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-artifacts)
  - Non-shareable artifacts need a "detailed" justification at submission, and reviewers must validate it. If reviewers disagree, authors must submit the artifacts or the paper is rejected.
  - Even then, the remainder (e.g., source code) must be shared publicly and permanently.
  - Restricted access (e.g., on Zenodo) is allowed only for sensitive artifacts. Restricting non-sensitive artifacts can rescind acceptance.
  - Contacts: sec27chairs@usenix.org and sec27aec@usenix.org.

### Inferences
- A NIDS paper using public datasets (CIC-IDS, UNSW-NB15, etc.) should release code, preprocessing scripts, trained models, and configurations.
- Proprietary or private traffic data can be withheld only with a detailed justification that reviewers accept. The code must still be released. Plan for a Zenodo deposit for the camera-ready.

### Gaps
- No explicit page cap is stated for the Open Science Appendix in 2027. NOT FOUND.

## Q5. Ethics appendix, human subjects, vulnerability disclosure

### Takeaway
- In 2027 the ethics appendix is strongly encouraged but no longer mandatory (it was mandatory in 2026).
- Reviewers judge ethics, and papers can be rejected on ethical grounds.
- For detailed requirements on human subjects, IRB, consent, and disclosure, the 2027 CFP points to the 2026 ethics guidelines. Those guidelines are quoted below and labelled as 2026.

### Cited Findings
- The 2027 CFP — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
  - "Reviewers will be asked to evaluate the ethics of both the research and the implications of all submissions." "Papers may be rejected on ethical grounds."
  - "Authors are strongly encouraged to include a well-labeled section in the Appendix (e.g., 'Ethics', 'Ethical Considerations', etc.)."
  - "Authors who do not include an adequate discussion of ethics may be asked to provide additional information."
  - The CFP refers authors to the "Ethics Guidelines from USENIX Security 2026."
- The 2027 CFP itself does not address human subjects or IRB. The "Human Aspects" topic lists usable security, policy/ethics, education, and online harms. — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- **[2026 reference, referenced by the '27 CFP]** In 2026, "All papers must contain an Ethical Considerations appendix" placed after the body and before the references, up to about one page. — [Sec '26 CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/call-for-papers#ethics)
  - The guidelines expect a stakeholder-based analysis covering:
    - stakeholders and their impacts
    - principles considered (Menlo Report: Beneficence, Respect for Persons, Justice, Respect for Law and Public Interest)
    - tangible and rights-based harms, mitigated and unmitigated
    - the decisions to conduct and to publish
    - team wellbeing
    - Terms of Service violations
  - "IRB approval is not a substitute for ethical analysis."
  - Participants "should be fully informed of the purposes and risks."
  - Deception must be justified and followed by debriefing.
  - "We expect researchers to disclose vulnerabilities as soon as they are discovered." Delay requires convincing ethical arguments. Authors should state to whom they disclosed and the outcome.

### Inferences
- A NIDS paper should still include a short ethics appendix. Topics to cover:
  - use of public or captured traffic, and any PII or payloads
  - dual use of evasion attacks
  - disclosure of any IDS bypass found in deployed products
- An ethics appendix counts toward the 20-page camera-ready budget.

### Gaps
- No 2027-specific human-subjects or IRB rules exist beyond the reference to the 2026 guidelines. NOT FOUND in the 2027 CFP.

## Q6. Topics (network security / IDS; ML for security vs. security of ML)

### Takeaway
"Intrusion and anomaly detection and prevention" is listed explicitly under Network security. USENIX's ML page says a paper proposing an ML method for intrusion detection must choose Network Security as its primary field, not "Security of ML."

### Cited Findings
- Main topic areas — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
  - System security
  - Network security
  - Security and privacy for web, mobile, and emerging technologies
  - Software security
  - Security of ML
  - Privacy of ML
  - Privacy and anonymity
  - Human aspects
  - Measurement
  - Hardware security
  - Applied cryptography
- Out-of-scope papers "may be rejected without full review." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Network security subtopics: "Intrusion and anomaly detection and prevention", "Network infrastructure security", "Denial-of-service attacks and countermeasures", "Wireless security", "Analysis of network and security protocols". — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Security of ML — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
  - "USENIX Security is a systems security venue. ML-focused contributions must be relevant to the broader systems security community."
  - "This topic is not meant for papers that propose/evaluate/improve ML-based techniques for a security task. Such papers should select the topic most closely related to the task."
- ML page guidance — [Submitting ML Work](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/submitting-ml-work-usenix-security)
  - A novel ML method for intrusion detection should take "Network Security" as its primary field.
  - An attack on a phishing detector belongs under Web Security.
  - An evaluation of ML for malware analysis belongs under Software Security.
  - Robustness work needs "a clear security flavour, presenting an adversary." Pure ML functionality or efficiency work is out of scope.
  - "Papers deemed out of scope will be desk rejected."
- "All papers submitted should provide a threat model that clearly articulates the (i) envisioned attacker(s), (ii) threat surfaces, (iii) generality, and (iv) practicality of the attack." — [Submitting ML Work](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/submitting-ml-work-usenix-security)

### Inferences
- An ML-based NIDS paper → primary topic Network Security.
- A paper attacking or defending ML-based NIDS (e.g., adversarial evasion) → Network Security, since it attacks a security detector. Choose Security of ML only if the contribution is about ML-specific vulnerabilities in general.
- Include an explicit threat model in either case.

### Gaps
- The ML page gives no guidance on datasets, baselines, or realistic evaluation. NOT FOUND.

## Q7. Review criteria, early reject, major revision, resubmission, LLM policy

### Takeaway
- Papers are judged on originality, relevance, scientific rigor, correctness, and clarity.
- Outcomes are Accept, Accept on Shepherd Approval, or Reject. The 2027 CFP has no "Major Revision" outcome.
- A paper rejected in Cycle 1 '27 cannot be resubmitted to Cycle 2 '27. Papers rejected from '26 may be submitted.
- AI tools are allowed, but hallucinated content counts as misconduct.

### Cited Findings
- "All submissions will be judged on originality, relevance, scientific rigor, correctness, and clarity. Submissions should be finished, complete papers." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Outcomes — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
  - "Accepted on Shepherd Approval": "some changes are needed" that are "highly unlikely to reduce their enthusiasm," including "text changes, clarifications, and explicit discussion of limitations."
  - "Rejected": reviews "will attempt to identify major changes that could move the paper toward a publishable state."
- "Papers rejected from either cycle of USENIX Security '26 may be submitted. Papers rejected from the first cycle of USENIX Security '27 may not be submitted to the second cycle of USENIX Security '27." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Desk rejection "for any reason they deem necessary, including, but not limited to: severe editorial issues, scope/fit issues, insufficient quality, fabricated references, failure to adhere to the CFP, etc." — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
- Early reject: only the notification dates are given (Oct 6, 2026; Mar 9, 2027), followed by rebuttal. — [CFP](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers)
- AI policy — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)
  - "The use of AI tools to assist in preparing submissions is permitted, and human authors are ultimately responsible for all submitted content and results."
  - Authors acknowledge "they have reviewed all content that was generated by AI as if they had written it themselves, including text, code, experimental data, and references."
  - "Fabrications or hallucinations, including non-existing references or incorrect authors, invented claims, and falsified results is considered academic misconduct and might lead to the paper being desk rejected or other sanctions."
  - Authors are encouraged to check with https://github.com/gianlucasb/hallucinator, the same tool the chairs use.
- The CFP links the USENIX Authorship Policy, which is new for '27: https://www.usenix.org/conferences/authorship-policy — [CFP PDF](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/sec27_cfp_110926.pdf)

### Inferences
- With no major-revision track, a Cycle 2 reject means waiting for USENIX Security '28 or going to another venue.
- Verify every reference before submission, for example by running hallucinator.

### Gaps
- Early-reject criteria and round structure (which papers advance) are not defined on the CFP. NOT FOUND.
- No requirement to describe changes made after a prior rejection. NOT FOUND.
- The USENIX Authorship Policy page was not fetched, so its contents were not reviewed.
