## A. Research identity
- Problem addressed: vulnerability of conformal prediction (CP) to training-time data poisoning that targets the *uncertainty* (set size) of specific victim samples rather than their labels.
- Central contribution (authors' words): "for the first time, we in this paper propose a new class of black-box data poisoning attacks against CP, where the adversary aims to cause the desired manipulations of some specific examples' prediction uncertainty results (instead of misclassifications)" (Abstract).
- Motivating limitation of prior work: prior adversarial-CP work addresses test-time attacks and focuses on "how to ensure the validity ... under the violations of the data exchangeability, and fail to consider the maliciously manipulated efficiency" (Sec. 1, p.2).
- What the paper claims is new: bi-level poisoning framework with relaxations for discrete sets and non-differentiable quantile; worst-case (sharpness-style) poisoned-model loss ℓ3; first/second-order influence-function closed-form updates avoiding retraining.

## B. Problem and threat model
- System protected: a classifier θ with split (and, briefly, full) CP using scores HPS, APS, RAPS, RSCP; target coverage 1-ε = 0.9.
- Attacker: training-data poisoner. Sec. 4.1: "no knowledge of the internal model parameters and the training process of the victim model, and is unable to alter test data ... cannot gain knowledge of the data points adopted for training, and can inject a limited number of manipulated new points into the training data." Has an auxiliary dataset D from the same distribution to train a surrogate θ*(D). White-box variant also "considered" (full access to training data and architecture).
- Attacker can observe / modify / inject / control: injects/replaces a ξ1 fraction of the training set (Sec. 4.2: "selects a subset Dp_tr from Dtr which takes an ξ1 ∈ [0,1] percentage of Dtr, and replaces it"). Reader note: this "replace a subset of Dtr" formulation sits uneasily with "cannot gain knowledge of the data points adopted for training"; the paper does not reconcile it (the formulation is run on the attacker's own auxiliary split).
- Can manipulate training data? Y — Sec. 4.1/4.2; budgets 0.5%–8% (Fig. 1), perturbation bound ϵ = 16/255 (L∞ image perturbation; Sec. 5 implementation details), clean-label style (labels kept correct for stealth).
- Can influence model updates? N beyond data — victim retrains from scratch on poisoned data (Sec. 5).
- Can manipulate benign traffic? N/A (image domain). Calibration set: NOT poisoned — calibration data assumed clean, exchangeable, correctly labeled (Sec. 3: "Let D = {Zi} denote a calibration set of exchangeable ... and correctly labeled examples").
- Knows detector / thresholds? Black-box: no (surrogate model only); white-box: yes. Knows CP score function and ε (used in loss Eq. 7).
- Explicitly excluded capabilities: altering test data; (black-box) model parameters and training data knowledge. Adaptivity over time / repeated poisoning: NOT PRESENT IN PAPER (one-shot poisoning).

## C. Technical mechanism
- Algorithm / architecture: optimise poison set D̃p to maximise set-size change for V target samples while keeping label prediction and true-label inclusion (stealth). Eq. 5 (high-level objective), Eq. 7 (hinge surrogate over calibration quantile index n_ε^ca = ⌈(1-ε)|D^ca|⌉), Eq. 8 (min-max over model perturbation ‖ζ‖≤ρ, SAM-style), Eq. 11/13 (first-order influence update with step τ), Eq. 17 (second-order Hessian update).
- Inputs → outputs: surrogate model θ*, subset Dp, target samples, ε → poisoned points D̃p.
- Mathematical formulation: Eq. 1 coverage P(Y ∈ Cε) ≥ 1-ε; Eq. 3 quantile with {∞}; Eq. 13 cosine alignment objective.
- Required assumptions: Assumption 3.1 exchangeability of calibration+test (stated); L locally convex & differentiable (Thm 4.1, stated); strictly convex & twice differentiable for second-order (stated, Sec. 4.3); attacker auxiliary data from same distribution (stated); victim retrains (implicit).
- Difference from earlier approaches (authors' claim): targets uncertainty not labels; closed-form updates vs. retraining-based MetaPoison.
- Guarantee type: FORMAL for the *attack optimisation* only, not for CP security.
  - Theorem 3.2 (Vovk et al. 2005): standard marginal coverage under exchangeability (cited).
  - Theorem 4.1: finite-sample upper bounds on L_ξ(θ*_{Dp→D̃p}) − L(θ*_u): first-order ≤ ϵ L_z |Dp| B_θ if τ ≤ 1/L1; second-order ≤ ϵ B_θ L_z |Dp| + (1 + ½ L1² L1)(ϵ L_z |Dp|)² (as printed; garbled in text extraction). Conditions: local convexity, Lipschitz gradients, bounded ‖θ*−θ*_u‖.
  - Theorem 4.2: under SGD with step conditions and Φ(Dp,θ_t) > μ, E ℓ3 decreases and lim ‖∇ℓ3(θ_t)‖ → 0 (stationary point of adversarial loss). Proof p.6-7.
  - Does NOT cover: any bound on achievable coverage loss or set-size distortion; calibration-set poisoning; non-exchangeable data.

## F. Limitations and unresolved questions
- Acknowledged by authors: future work on more models, CP methods, larger datasets, and robust CP defenses (Sec. 6). Full CP: perturbation-based poisons "would violate the data exchangeability assumption" and risk detection by checking coverage (p.7).
- Untested assumptions (reader): calibration set clean; attacker has same-distribution auxiliary data; only images; results are empirical, not bounds.
- Under temporal shift: NOT ADDRESSED.
- Under adversarial manipulation: this paper IS the manipulation; it is per-target-sample (targeted) set-size manipulation, not marginal coverage destruction.
- Under contaminated update stream: one-shot training poisoning only; no repeated/online retraining.
- When unseen attacks resemble benign traffic: N/A.
- Guarantees that disappear: the paper's key point is that marginal coverage *does not* disappear (stealth) while target-sample efficiency/conditional behaviour is manipulated — i.e., marginal coverage ≠ protection against targeted manipulation.

## G. Relationship to the investigated NIDS problem
- Which parts it shows failing: demonstrates (empirically, images, not NIDS) that poisoning the *training* data of a split-CP pipeline can shift per-target uncertainty while marginal coverage stays near 1-ε. NIDS analogue (reader inference, not shown): an attacker poisoning the detector's training data could make specific attack flows look confidently benign (overconfidence) without moving the calibrated aggregate false-alarm rate — so a coverage/FPR audit on benign data would not reveal the poisoning. It does NOT attack the calibration set, does NOT involve drift, and gives no defense.
- It does NOT solve calibrating an NIDS false-alarm threshold on a contaminated, drifting benign stream: it assumes the calibration set is clean and exchangeable, the setting is static, one-shot, image classification. No online updates, no adaptive contamination over time, no protocol constraints on perturbations (perturbations are L∞ ≤ 16/255 pixel noise; a protocol-valid flow feature space is not considered).
- Evidence that could INVALIDATE the hypothesis or a candidate gap: none invalidates; supports the gap claim that marginal CP guarantees do not protect against targeted training poisoning. Note "proposed attack ≠ demonstrated in NIDS".
- Closest papers: CP-009 (provable defense, training + calibration poisoning), CP-011 (Byzantine federated CP), CP-012 (robust CP vs evasion/poisoning), CP-028 (contaminated reference data).
